# A. L. S. Tutor — Course 1200710 Layered-Intelligence Curriculum

**Build state:** Implemented curriculum and instructional architecture baseline  
**Course:** Florida Mathematics for College Algebra, 1200710  
**Learner:** High-school student preparing for college algebra  
**Role:** Supplemental tutor; the learner's teacher, assignments, and current class sequence control day-to-day priorities

## 1. Purpose

A. L. S. Tutor is not a general chatbot with algebra prompts. It is a course-specific instructional system designed to help a learner understand mathematics, explain her reasoning, recover missing prerequisite skills, practice independently, and prepare for later college mathematics.

The system combines reviewed curriculum, deterministic practice, misconception diagnosis, adaptive routing, spaced review, culturally responsive instruction, and a bounded generative-AI explanation layer. The essential course remains usable when the AI layer is unavailable or its free allowance is exhausted.

## 2. Governing instructional foundations

### 2.1 Algebra Project five-step process

Each new or difficult concept should move through:

1. **Concrete event:** start with a familiar experience or observable situation.
2. **Picture/model:** represent that experience with a drawing, table, number line, graph, area model, or diagram.
3. **People Talk:** invite the learner to describe what happened in her own words.
4. **Feature Talk:** identify the quantities, patterns, changes, and relationships that matter.
5. **Symbolic representation:** translate the learner's understanding into conventional mathematical notation.

The tutor must not rush directly to symbols when the learner lacks a conceptual anchor.

### 2.2 ICUCARE-informed equity behavior

The tutor:

- includes the learner's voice in the mathematical conversation;
- treats partial reasoning as evidence to examine rather than failure to punish;
- uses meaningful contexts without assuming that every Black learner has the same interests or experiences;
- maintains rigorous expectations and supplies the support required to meet them;
- presents more than one representation when the first explanation does not connect;
- recognizes and names mathematical competence specifically;
- gives the learner agency to choose an example, representation, or next practice path;
- avoids deficit language, empty praise, stereotypes, and performative cultural references.

### 2.3 Culturally relevant pedagogy

Every unit must support:

- **Academic success:** accurate course-level mathematics and increasing independent performance.
- **Cultural competence:** respect for the learner's language, identity, family, and community knowledge.
- **Critical consciousness:** opportunities to interpret quantitative claims, costs, wages, credit, data, access, and other real conditions through mathematics.

Cultural relevance is a bridge to rigorous mathematics, never a replacement for it.

### 2.4 Calculus Project acceleration principles

The system uses:

- high expectations;
- preview-before-class opportunities;
- prerequisite repair without assigning a remedial identity;
- visible progression toward advanced mathematics;
- cumulative review;
- family-readable evidence;
- an explicit identity message: the learner is developing as a mathematician.

## 3. Layered intelligence architecture

| Layer | Function | Cost posture |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Course authority | Defines the approved 1200710 scope and sequence | Local, unlimited |
| 2. Prerequisite map | Detects missing number, fraction, sign, distribution, and notation skills | Local, unlimited |
| 3. Concept instruction | Supplies concrete event, model, natural-language explanation, features, and symbols | Local, unlimited |
| 4. Deterministic mathematics | Generates supported problems, checks answers, and verifies known transformations | Local, unlimited |
| 5. Misconception diagnosis | Connects common errors to targeted reteaching | Local, unlimited |
| 6. Adaptive routing | Chooses prerequisite, current lesson, supported practice, or review | Local, unlimited |
| 7. Learning evidence | Records attempts, correct answers, hints, checkpoints, and review dates | Local, unlimited |
| 8. Cultural/context intelligence | Selects meaningful contexts while preserving learner choice and mathematical rigor | Local, unlimited |
| 9. Conversational AI | Interprets unusual wording and supplies a personalized explanation when local layers cannot | Free allocation only; bounded and fail-closed |
| 10. Governance | Prevents AI from grading, claiming mastery, inventing curriculum, or exposing secrets | Deterministic |

Generative AI is the exception layer. It does not own curriculum, scoring, mastery, progress, or answer truth.

## 4. Teaching exchange contract

For an instructional problem, the tutor follows this order:

1. Establish the exact problem and the learner's current step.
2. Ask what she notices or already understands.
3. Diagnose the concept or prerequisite involved.
4. Select a concrete context or visual representation when needed.
5. Explain one mathematical move and why it is valid.
6. Ask the learner to perform the next move.
7. Validate the reasoning deterministically where supported.
8. If incorrect, classify the error and provide one increasingly specific hint.
9. After two supported attempts—or an explicit request—show a complete worked solution.
10. Check the result in the original problem.
11. Give a near-transfer problem to determine whether the learner can repeat the reasoning.
12. Schedule review based on evidence, not confidence alone.

## 5. Course sequence

### Phase A — Readiness and algebra foundations

#### Unit A1: Number sense and operation structure

- Signed numbers and absolute value
- Fractions, decimals, ratios, and percent
- Order of operations and grouping
- Properties of real numbers
- Estimation and reasonableness

**Common misconceptions:** treating subtraction as commutative; losing negative signs; adding denominators; applying operation order strictly left-to-right.

**Evidence:** learner explains signs and operation order, completes mixed calculations, and identifies an unreasonable result.

#### Unit A2: Algebraic language

- Variables, constants, coefficients, and terms
- Like terms
- Distributive property
- Translating words, tables, and expressions
- Evaluating expressions by substitution

**Common misconceptions:** combining unlike terms; distributing to only the first term; dropping parentheses around negative substitutions.

### Phase B — Expressions

#### Unit B1: Exponents and radicals

- Integer-exponent rules
- Zero and negative exponents
- Rational exponents
- Radical notation and exact values
- Simplifying appropriate radical expressions

**Concept path:** repeated multiplication → exponent table → pattern extension → formal law.

#### Unit B2: Polynomial expressions

- Degree and standard form
- Addition and subtraction
- Multiplication and special products
- Factoring greatest common factors and common patterns
- Polynomial division at the course level

**Concept path:** area model → multiplication grid → feature relationships → symbolic multiplication/factoring.

#### Unit B3: Rational expressions

- Domain restrictions
- Factoring before simplifying
- Multiplication and division
- Addition and subtraction with denominators
- Distinguishing factors from terms

### Phase C — Equations and inequalities

#### Unit C1: Linear equations and formulas

- One-, two-, and multi-step equations
- Variables on both sides
- Literal equations and formula rearrangement
- Proportions and contextual equations
- Solution checking

**Concept path:** physical balance → annotated balance drawing → natural-language inverse operations → equality-preserving transformations.

#### Unit C2: Inequalities and absolute value

- Linear and compound inequalities
- Interval and number-line representation
- Effect of multiplication/division by a negative
- Absolute-value equations and inequalities as distance
- Contextual interpretation

#### Unit C3: Rational and radical equations

- Restrictions before transformation
- Clearing denominators
- Isolating radicals
- Squaring and extraneous solutions
- Verification in the original equation

#### Unit C4: Exponential and logarithmic equations

- Equivalent exponential and logarithmic forms
- Solving common-base equations
- Logarithms as unknown exponents
- Growth and decay equations
- Domain checks

### Phase D — Functions and modeling

#### Unit D1: Function foundations

- Function definition
- Function notation
- Domain and range
- Tables, graphs, equations, and verbal descriptions
- Intercepts, intervals, extrema, and average rate of change

#### Unit D2: Linear functions

- Slope as rate of change
- Slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms
- Parallel and perpendicular relationships
- Linear modeling and interpretation
- Arithmetic sequences as linear patterns

**Context choices:** wages and hours, savings, travel, service plans, production, sports statistics, or another learner-selected context.

#### Unit D3: Systems

- Graphing, substitution, and elimination
- One, none, or infinitely many solutions
- Systems of inequalities
- Feasible regions
- Contextual meaning of an intersection

#### Unit D4: Quadratic functions

- Standard, factored, and vertex forms
- Zeros, intercepts, vertex, and axis of symmetry
- Factoring and square-root methods
- Completing the square
- Quadratic formula and discriminant
- Quadratic modeling

#### Unit D5: Piecewise and absolute-value functions

- Reading input conditions
- Evaluating the correct branch
- Graphing boundaries
- Step and absolute-value models
- Interpretation with units

#### Unit D6: Exponential and logarithmic functions

- Constant ratio versus constant difference
- Growth and decay factors
- Initial value and rate
- Inverse relationship with logarithms
- Contextual modeling and limits

### Phase E — Function operations

#### Unit E1: Composite functions

- Function operations
- Evaluating from the inside out
- Domain considerations
- Contextual chains of processes

#### Unit E2: Inverse functions

- Reversing input-output relationships
- Finding an inverse algebraically
- One-to-one restrictions
- Checking through composition

### Phase F — Integration and readiness

- Mixed retrieval without an example first
- Explanation of method selection
- Error analysis
- Multiple representations of the same relationship
- College-style notation and vocabulary
- Cumulative personalized reteaching
- Final readiness evidence without unsupported pass/fail claims

## 6. Thirty-six-week pacing frame

The application contains a 36-week guide organized around:

1. Weeks 1–9: foundations, exponents, polynomials, rational expressions.
2. Weeks 10–18: equations, inequalities, and function foundations.
3. Weeks 19–27: graphs, systems, piecewise models, and quadratics.
4. Weeks 28–36: quadratic interpretation, exponential/logarithmic functions, composition, inverses, modeling, and cumulative review.

This is a pacing guide, not a deadline. The current teacher's assignments control priority, and the learner may remain on a concept until prerequisite and near-transfer evidence are adequate.

## 7. Misconception-to-intervention rules

| Observed error | Likely need | Tutor intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Loses a negative sign | Signed-number structure | Number line, sign annotation, one-operation rehearsal |
| Adds unlike terms | Term classification | Color/underline variable parts before combining |
| Distributes to one term only | Multiplicative structure | Area model or arrows to every term |
| “Moves” terms without justification | Equality meaning | Balance model and explicit inverse operation on both sides |
| Cancels terms across addition | Factor/term distinction | Factor first; show cancellation as division by a common factor |
| Uses wrong function branch | Piecewise conditions | Read and highlight the input condition before substitution |
| Drops ± in quadratics | Multiple solutions | Connect two x-intercepts with two algebraic candidates |
| Accepts an extraneous answer | Original-equation authority | Mandatory substitution check |
| Confuses f⁻¹ with reciprocal | Function inversion | Input-output machine reversed versus numerical reciprocal |
| Calls exponential growth linear | Pattern recognition | Compare differences and ratios in a table |

## 8. Mastery and learning evidence

The application may record:

- concept attempted;
- question and supported answer type;
- first-try correctness;
- number and level of hints;
- whether a complete solution was revealed;
- independent near-transfer result;
- checkpoint status;
- review due date;
- focused study time.

The system must not claim full mastery from one answer. A recommended internal progression is:

1. **Encountered:** lesson opened or concept discussed.
2. **Supported:** learner succeeds with a model or hint.
3. **Developing:** learner succeeds independently on the same form.
4. **Transfer:** learner succeeds on a changed representation or context.
5. **Retained:** learner succeeds again after spaced delay.

Only deterministic evidence advances these states.

## 9. AI cost and escalation policy

### Local-first rule

Every tutor message first enters the local layered-intelligence engine. The local engine handles:

- concept explanations;
- course vocabulary;
- common hints;
- known misconceptions;
- supported equation patterns;
- supported factorization patterns;
- navigation to a lesson or practice path;
- mastery and review decisions.

### AI escalation conditions

Generative AI may be used only when:

- the learner supplies an unusual or multi-part problem the deterministic engine cannot parse;
- the learner needs a rephrased explanation beyond reviewed variants;
- ambiguity requires conversational interpretation;
- the request remains within course 1200710 or its prerequisites.

AI must not be called merely because the learner asks a broad concept question already covered by the curriculum.

### Cost controls

- Cloudflare Workers AI free allocation only.
- No OpenAI API key and no paid fallback.
- Hard shared daily model-request ceiling.
- Per-IP short-window rate limit.
- Short context and output limits.
- When the allowance is unavailable, the tutor returns to reviewed lessons, deterministic practice, and local explanations.
- No billing upgrade, prepaid credit, or paid overage is authorized by this curriculum.

The hard request ceiling is a last-resort financial guard, not the learner's daily question limit. Local curriculum intelligence remains available beyond that ceiling.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

The curriculum build passes when:

1. A broad course question is answered locally without consuming AI allocation.
2. A supported equation produces a hint before revealing the complete solution.
3. A complete solution identifies each transformation and checks the result.
4. An unsupported exact problem escalates to AI only after local classification.
5. Exhausted AI capacity does not disable lessons, practice, diagnostics, progress, or local concept explanations.
6. Deterministic logic—not the model—controls scoring and evidence.
7. Explanations reflect the five-step concrete-to-symbolic process where appropriate.
8. The tutor sustains rigor while respecting the learner's voice and identity.
9. No cultural context is imposed as a stereotype.
10. No provider credential is present in public source.
